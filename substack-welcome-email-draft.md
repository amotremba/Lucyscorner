publication's website — so it's the only thing a new subscriber gets before

## Body

Hi — welcome. You just subscribed to Lucy's Corner, which is a newsletter
about a 6-year-old Cocker Spaniel with strong opinions about squirrels.

I'm Anne. This is Lucy's.

She was adopted in July 2023, a little timid, and it did not last long. These
days she runs the household, patrols the yard for squirrels she will never
catch, and has strong feelings about anyone who knocks on the door. I write
about her adventures; she supplies the material.

A few places to start:

**The Squirrel She Never Caught** — 22 minutes of pursuit, zero catches.
[The Staring Contest With a Deer](https://amotremba.github.io/Lucyscorner/blog/2026/08/31/the-staring-contest-with-a-deer/)
— she has faced down a fawn and won.
[Her favorite things](https://amotremba.github.io/Lucyscorner/#shop) — some of
these are affiliate links, which fund the whole operation. Lucy gets a treat,
at no extra cost to you.

One question, if you feel like it: **how did you find me?** A reply is always
welcome, and it helps these emails land in your inbox rather than the spam
folder.

Talk soon,
Anne

## Notes on this draft

**Word count:** ~140, inside the 50–150 range where welcome emails perform best.

**One photo is a judgment call.** I left the body text-only. Image-heavy emails
load slower and land in promotions more often, and welcome emails are the
highest-performing email you'll send — worth keeping it lean. If you'd rather
have one image, `websitelucy.jpg` is the natural choice (200, verified):

    ![Lucy](/websitelucy.jpg)

Substack needs an absolute URL for externally-hosted images, so use:

    ![Lucy](https://amotremba.github.io/Lucyscorner/websitelucy.jpg)

**Why the reply question is there.** Asking subscribers to reply creates a
two-way thread, which measurably improves deliverability. Worth keeping even
though most people won't answer.

**The shop link is deliberate.** It's the referral engine for the affiliate
revenue, and framing it as "funds the whole operation" reads as disclosure
without being a wall of legal text.

**Two posts linked, not six.** A welcome email isn't an archive. Two gives a
sense of voice without asking for a commitment. If you'd rather send a digest
occasionally, that's a separate thing — the welcome email shouldn't do that job.

**Subject line alternatives:**
- Welcome to Lucy's Corner 🐾
- New here? Lucy has opinions
- Meet Lucy (she has opinions about squirrels)
