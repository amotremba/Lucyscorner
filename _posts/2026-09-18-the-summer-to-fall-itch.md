Somewhere between the last hot afternoon and the first cool morning, Lucy's scratching changes its whole personality. Summer scratching is quick and specific — a flea patrol behind one ear, a grass-seed investigation between the toes. By the time the leaves start turning, it's slower and more constant, the kind of scratch that means her skin has gone dry and cranky right along with the weather.

We didn't clock this pattern until her third fall with us. Now it's just part of the calendar: the itch changes shape, so the routine has to change with it.

![Lucy lying in a pile of orange and red fall leaves](/Lucyscorner/lucyinleaves.jpg)

## The summer hangover

Summer allergies don't actually end on the first cool day — they linger. Grass, ragweed, and whatever pollen is still airborne in September keep irritating a dog's skin well past the point the humans have moved on to sweater weather. The fix isn't complicated: keep up the after-walk wipe-down, keep flea and tick prevention going through the transition instead of stopping the day it feels cooler out, and don't assume "it's fall now" means allergy season is over.

## The fall arrival

Then the actual autumn problem shows up: the air dries out, and so does everything attached to a dog. Coats get flakier, skin gets tighter, and an itch that was "seasonal allergy" in September starts looking more like "just needs moisture" by October. This part is less about fighting something and more about repairing — hydrating the coat and skin barrier instead of just managing an irritant.
